### Paste into Video Overview → Explainer → Customize

> Create a **6–8 minute target** narrated video explainer for mixed-grade high-school students (grades 9–12), including English learners, titled **"When AI Gets It Wrong: Smart Choices, Real Consequences."** This is the LAST Edpuzzle for the AI Basics Passport and a friendly invitation to the AI Ethics Badge, where students will investigate real, documented cases.
>
> Use ONLY the selected sources in this notebook. Do not fabricate incidents, statistics, quotations, dates, organizations, legal outcomes, or case details. If a named case lacks a verified source, leave it out rather than guess. Keep each real-world incident factual, compassionate, and age-appropriate. Distinguish what a documented case PROVES from risks students should consider. Do not show graphic incidents or sensational headlines; do not make AI sound either perfectly safe or inevitably dangerous.
>
> **Visual direction:** Polished educational documentary; midnight navy, vivid electric blue, teal and white, consistent with our AI Passport site. Strong visual storytelling, legible text, clean diagrams, illustrated scenarios and captions; no crowded slides or cartoonishly childish presentation.
>
> **Narrative structure:**
> 1. **Hook (about 30 seconds):** AI can help us learn, create, and solve problems. But sounding confident is not the same as being correct. Introduce the question, "Who checks what happens next?"
> 2. **Remember AI Basics (about 45 seconds):** Recap that AI can make mistakes, invent details, reflect bias, and should SUPPORT rather than replace human thinking. Give a concrete classroom example of verifying a source.
> 3. **Four case-study teasers (about 3 minutes total):** From the source packet, give a brief, accurate "What happened?" hook about (a) AI-generated business scams or fake reviews; (b) an AI companion and boundaries; (c) autonomous driving and safety; and (d) biased hiring decisions. For each, say who might be affected and the question students will explore. Don't resolve the entire case; students will do that in their Ethics Badge.
> 4. **Social and personal safety (about 1 minute):** Explain privacy, personal information, realistic media/deepfakes, helpful versus unhealthy dependence on AI companions, and when to consult a trusted adult. Avoid implying every chatbot interaction is dangerous. Include at least one practical protective action.
> 5. **Student decision toolkit (about 1 minute):** "PAUSE: What am I being asked to trust? CHECK: What independent evidence supports it? CHOOSE: What is the safe, fair, responsible human action?" Model with a short non-graphic example.
> 6. **Bridge to badges (about 45 seconds):** The AI Basics reflection asks students to name a risk, something AI should not do for them, an answer to fact-check, and a responsible-use rule. Their next AI Ethics Badge goes further: students will investigate documented incidents, explain who was affected, identify what went wrong, and propose a human rule that might prevent harm. End with: **"AI literacy means knowing what AI can do. AI ethics means deciding what we should do with it."**
>
> Voice: thoughtful, engaging, calm, clear, age-respectful. Define **bias, hallucination, privacy, deepfake, and accountability** briefly in accessible language. Prefer about 100–130 spoken words per minute, natural pauses, and short sections. Avoid fear-mongering, political persuasion, dramatic music, or direct advice to students about diagnosing people or trusting an AI for emotional support.
>
> This is an **introduction**, NOT the Ethics Badge case-study assignment or the AI Basics certification. Do not tell students they have earned either badge by watching.

### Add these three Edpuzzle pauses to the generated video

1. **After the case teasers — Multiple choice:** "An AI tool gives a convincing answer or recommendation. What should you do before acting on it?" **Correct:** Check a reliable independent source or ask a qualified person. **Distractors:** Trust it because it sounds certain; share it immediately; assume a citation always proves it.
2. **After the privacy/boundaries example — Multiple choice:** "A chatbot asks for a private detail you would not post publicly. What is the safest next step?" **Correct:** Do not share that detail; ask a trusted adult if you need help. **Distractors:** Share it because the bot is friendly; post it in a group chat; assume the data cannot be saved.
3. **At the ending — Open response (1–2 sentences):** "Which AI risk or real-world case would you want to investigate in the Ethics Badge, and what question would you ask?" **Formative only:** Any relevant risk/case + thoughtful investigable question.

**Production & publishing checklist:** Generate via NotebookLM/Gemini Notebook Video Overview using teacher-vetted case-study documents; fact-check the video against sources (AI-generated videos can be wrong); confirm case sensitivity and accessibility; export the video if available and import/assign it in Edpuzzle; add three pauses at matching scene transitions rather than made-up timestamps; publish the real student-facing Edpuzzle link to Classroom and the Passport site only after available. **Do not place a placeholder link that appears playable.** Original case packets, final reflection and 25-question certification remain the authoritative assignments.
